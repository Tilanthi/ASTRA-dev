## Critical Findings for Our Contradiction

### 1. "Fray and Fragment" Scenario

**Key Result**: Large filaments form first, then fragment ("fray") into velocity-coherent fibers (subfilaments). These fibers then fragment to form cores.

**Relevance**: This is the **exact framework** needed to resolve the supercritical contradiction. Cores observed in supercritical HGBS filaments formed during an earlier near-critical phase or in embedded near-critical fibers.

**Hierarchical process**:
```
Large-scale cloud (10^6 M☉)
    ↓ (forms)
Pc-scale regions
    ↓ (fragment into)
Velocity-coherent filaments (~0.5 pc long)
    ↓ (quasi-statically fragment into)
Dense cores (~0.25 pc spacing)
```

### 2. Fiber/Sub-Filament Properties (CRITICAL QUANTITATIVE DATA)

**Widths**: ~0.1 pc (sonic length scale)
**Lengths**: ~0.5 pc
**Mass-per-unit-length**: **Below critical threshold** (<10 M☉/pc for isothermal cylinders)
**Core spacing**: ~0.25 pc (order of filament width)
**Stability**: Sub-critical (stable against gravitational collapse)

**CRITICAL INSIGHT**: If fibers have W_fiber ≈ 0.1 pc and λ_fiber ≈ 0.25 pc, then:
```
λ_fiber/W_fiber ≈ 0.25/0.1 ≈ 2.5
```

This is **remarkably close to HGBS PM λ/W ≈ 2.79**!

### 3. Velocity-Coherent Structures

**Definition**: Filaments identified by velocity coherence via 13CO emission analysis.

**Methodology**:
- Gaussian fitting to 13CO spectra reveals multiple velocity components
- Position-position-velocity space analysis detects velocity-coherent structures
- Automated algorithm (FIVE: Friends in Velocity) identifies filaments

**Result**: 35 filaments identified in L1495/B213 region

### 4. Two-Step Core Formation Process

**Step 1**: Velocity-coherent filaments form first from quiescent, subsonic gas
**Step 2**: Dense cores fragment from these filaments via gravitational collapse

**Key insight**: Turbulent dissipation **precedes** core formation. The transition between large-scale (filament) and small-scale (core) velocity regimes involves minimal kinematic changes (no shock compression).

### 5. Bundle Formation

**Finding**: Filaments group into "bundles" (families) on large scales.

**Relevance**: This provides the missing link between fiber-level and filament-level observations. HGBS filaments may actually be bundles of velocity-coherent fibers.

## Implications for Our Paper

### 1. Quantitative λ/W Explanation

**Smith+2016 measurements**:
- Fiber width: W_fiber ≈ 0.1 pc
- Fiber core spacing: λ_fiber ≈ 0.25 pc
- Fiber λ/W: ≈ 2.5

**HGBS measurements**:
- Filament width: W_filament ≈ 0.1 pc (assumed)
- PM λ/W: ≈ 2.79
- NN λ/W: ≈ 1.01

**Resolution**: The PM λ/W ≈ 2.79 matches the fiber-scale λ/W ≈ 2.5! This suggests PM is measuring the fiber-scale spacing, not the filament-scale spacing.

### 2. Why NN λ/W < 1.25

**Explanation**: NN measures the mixture of multiple fiber spacings within a filament. If different fibers have slightly different spacings and velocities, the NN statistic will compress the observed ratio.

**Alternatively**: NN may be measuring something else entirely (migration, selection effects, or geometric artifacts).

### 3. Resolution of Supercritical Contradiction

**Smith+2016 framework**:
1. Large filaments form first (may be supercritical)
2. Filaments fray into near-critical fibers (sub-critical mass-per-unit-length)
3. Fibers fragment to form cores

**Application to HGBS**:
- HGBS supercritical filaments (f ≥ 1.5) contain embedded near-critical fibers
- Cores form in fibers, not in parent filament
- Parent filament provides gravitational potential but not fragmentation physics

### 4. Filament Width Assumption

**Critical insight**: Smith+2016 shows fiber width ≈ 0.1 pc, which is the **same as HGBS filament width assumption**.

**Implication**: If fibers and filaments have similar widths, then the λ/W compression cannot be explained by width differences alone. The compression must come from other effects:
- Projection of multiple spacings
- Velocity coherence effects
- Statistical mixing

## Observational Details

**Data**:
- 13CO(1-0), N2H+(1-0), and SO/N2H+(3-2) lines with 14 m FCRAO telescope

- Active star-forming region

- Spans the full star-formation sequence

**Core population**: 19 dense cores (some starless, some protostellar)
- Not uniformly distributed
- Clustered in groups with separations ~0.25 pc

## Comparison with Other Studies

### Smith+2016 vs Hacar+2013

**Smith+2016**:
- Focuses on L1495/B213 region
- Emphasizes "fray and fragment" scenario
- Identifies 35 velocity-coherent filaments

**Hacar+2013** (same region):
- First to identify velocity-coherent structures in Taurus
- Proposed two-step formation process
- Established filament properties

**Consistency**: Both studies find similar filament properties and support hierarchical fragmentation.

### Smith+2016 vs Yang+2024

**Smith+2016** (Taurus, low-mass):
- Fiber width: ~0.1 pc
- Fiber length: ~0.5 pc
- Core spacing: ~0.25 pc

**Yang+2024** (Orion B, intermediate-mass):
- Fiber width: 1000-2000 au (median: ~1400 au = ~0.007 pc)
- Fiber length: 3000-9000 au (median: ~4500 au = ~0.02 pc)
- Core spacing: 900-2300 au

**Difference**: Yang+2024 fibers are **smaller** than Smith+2016 fibers by factor of ~10-20.

**Implication**: Fiber properties may vary with environment (low-mass vs. intermediate-mass regions). This complicates direct quantitative comparisons.
