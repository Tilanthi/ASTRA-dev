## Referee's Critique (Summary)

The referee identified a critical issue: **The paper's main conclusion (regional diversity reflects magnetic field geometry) rests entirely on the PM statistic, yet the paper demonstrates PM may be unreliable.**

Key points:
1. Synthetic test shows PM → L/3 for simple periodic filaments (>900% bias)
2. Argument that "HGBS filaments are not simple" is circular
3. NN/PM discrepancy (factor 2-3) is not a minor caveat—it's THE central issue
4. The discrepancy discussion was buried across multiple sections
5. Referee requested: Dedicated section + commit to one interpretation OR present two scenarios

---

## Restructuring Approach

**Instead of defending PM**, I restructured the paper to make the NN/PM discrepancy the CENTRAL FINDING:

1. **Abstract rewritten**: Leads with the discrepancy, presents two scenarios
2. **New dedicated section**: Section 3 "THE NN/PM DISCREPANCY: TWO COMPETING INTERPRETATIONS"
3. **Conclusions reframed**: Ambiguity IS the result, not a caveat
4. **Both interpretations presented fairly** as viable scenarios

## Summary

The referee was right: The NN/PM discrepancy is not a minor caveat—it's THE central issue.

Rather than defending PM or trying to resolve the ambiguity, I've restructured the paper to:
1. Make the discrepancy the central finding
2. Present both interpretations as viable scenarios
3. Acknowledge that resolving this ambiguity requires additional fiber-resolved observations

This is more scientifically honest than claiming PM is correct when we know PM is unreliable for simple periodic filaments and NN is below the theoretical minimum.

The paper no longer claims "magnetic field geometry drives regional diversity" as a definitive result. Instead, it presents two scenarios and acknowledges both remain viable until fiber-resolved observations resolve which statistic captures the true fragmentation scale.

**This is not a weakness—it's scientific honesty about the current state of knowledge.**
