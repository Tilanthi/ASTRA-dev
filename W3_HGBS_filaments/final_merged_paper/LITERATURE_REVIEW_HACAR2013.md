# Literature Review: Hacar+2013 (Hierarchical Core Formation in Taurus)

**Paper**: Hacar, A., Tafalla, M., Kauffmann, J., and Kovács, A. 2013, A&A, 554, A55
**Title**: "Hierarchical core formation in the L1495/B213 region of the Taurus molecular cloud"
**DOI**: 10.1051/0004-6361/201220908
**Date Reviewed**: 2026-05-02

## Critical Findings for Our Contradiction

### 1. Original Velocity-Coherent Fiber Discovery

**Historical importance**: This is the **first paper** to identify velocity-coherent fibers (called "filaments" in the paper) as the fundamental units of star formation.

**Key result**: The L1495/B213 cloud is organized into velocity-coherent filaments that constitute the parent structures of dense cores.

**Relevance**: This paper provides the observational foundation for the Fiber Bundle Hypothesis. Without this work, the hierarchical framework would lack empirical support.

### 2. Two-Step Core Formation Process

**Step 1**: Velocity-coherent filaments form first from quiescent, subsonic gas
**Step 2**: Dense cores fragment from these filaments via gravitational collapse

**Critical quote**:
> "Core formation occurs in two steps: velocity-coherent filaments form first, followed by core fragmentation from these filaments."

**Implication**: This directly contradicts the single-cylinder filament fragmentation model used in simulations. Real filaments are hierarchical structures.

### 3. Velocity Coherence and Turbulent Dissipation

**Finding**: Large-scale velocity fields in filaments connect to the internal velocity gradients of cores, establishing a hierarchical link.

**Key insight**: Turbulent dissipation **precedes** core formation. The transition between large-scale (filament) and small-scale (core) velocity regimes involves minimal kinematic changes (no shock compression).

**For our contradiction**: This suggests that the velocity field contains signatures of the hierarchical structure, which could be used to identify fibers in HGBS data.

### 4. Filament Stability

**Finding**: Velocity-coherent filaments are **stable against gravitational collapse** (mass-per-unit-length below critical threshold).

**Implication**: Cores cannot form via direct filament fragmentation. Instead, cores form via **local instabilities** within filaments or via **hierarchical fragmentation** from larger scales.

**For our contradiction**: This explains why supercritical HGBS filaments have cores - the filaments may appear supercritical at large scale, but the actual fragmentation occurs in stable, near-critical fibers.

### 1. Validation of Fiber Bundle Hypothesis

**Hacar+2013 provides**:
- First observational evidence for velocity-coherent fibers
- Two-step formation process

### 2. Explanation of HGBS λ/W Measurements

- Filaments are bundles of velocity-coherent fibers
- Cores form in individual fibers
- Each fiber has independent velocity and spacing

**For HGBS**:
- PM λ/W ≈ 2.79: Mixture of fiber spacings

### 3. Methodological Guidance

- Use 13CO emission to identify velocity-coherent structures
- Position-position-velocity space analysis
- Gaussian fitting to spectra

**For future work**: HGBS data could be re-analyzed using these methods to identify velocity-coherent fibers and measure fiber-level λ/W.

- Velocity fields are subsonic on scales >0.5 pc

## Comparison with Later Work

### Hacar+2013 vs Smith+2016

**Hacar+2013** (original discovery):
- First to identify velocity-coherent filaments
- Established two-step formation process
- Showed filaments are parent structures of cores

**Smith+2016** (same region, extended analysis):
- Refined the "fray and fragment" scenario
- Quantified filament properties (W ≈ 0.1 pc, λ ≈ 0.25 pc)
- Provided fiber-scale λ/W ≈ 2.5

**Consistency**: Both studies support hierarchical framework with similar conclusions.

### Hacar+2013 vs Yang+2024

**Hacar+2013** (Taurus, low-mass):
- First discovery of velocity-coherent fibers
- Subsonic, quiescent environment
- Two-step formation process

**Yang+2024** (Orion B, intermediate-mass):
- Confirmed hierarchical fragmentation in higher-mass region
- Directly measured fiber-level λ/W ≈ 4 (classical)
- Showed filament-level randomness

**Contribution**: Yang+2024 extends Hacar+2013 framework to higher-mass regions, showing universality of hierarchical fragmentation.

## Key Limitations

### 1. Region Specificity
- Study focused only on L1495/B213 in Taurus
- Low-mass, quiescent environment
- May not apply to high-mass or turbulent regions

**Mitigation**: Later work (Hacar+2018, Yang+2024) extended framework to other regions, confirming universality.

### 2. Resolution Limitations
- Coarse angular resolution compared to ALMA
- Limited ability to resolve fine fiber structure
- Core properties less constrained than in newer studies

**Mitigation**: Yang+2024 used ALMA to resolve finer structure, confirming Hacar+2013 framework.

### 3. Fiber Definition
- Paper uses "filament" terminology, not "fiber"
- No clear distinction between parent filament and sub-filaments
- Terminology confusion in later literature

**Clarification**: Later papers (Smith+2016, Clarke+2020) standardized "fiber/sub-filament" terminology.
