# Peer Review Improvements Implemented

## Summary of Changes

This document summarizes the improvements made to address the credible concerns from the aggressive peer review, while rejecting unreasonable demands that would invalidate standard HGBS methodology.

---

## Changes Implemented

### 1. Three-Level Hierarchy Toned Down (Issue #7)

**Before**: Presented as a universal physical law with "three-level fragmentation hierarchy"

**After**: Presented as an interesting finding from ONE region only:

**Abstract change**:
- From: "...suggesting a three-level fragmentation hierarchy"
- To: "A recent fiber-resolved analysis in Orion B found that fiber-to-core spacing recovers the classical 4× prediction... This single-region result suggests that hierarchical structure may influence measured spacings, but generalization to other HGBS regions requires fiber identification data that are not currently available."

**Hierarchical interpretation section**:
- Added "Critical caveat: This interpretation is based on ONE region only"
- Changed: "should NOT be generalized to other HGBS regions without fiber identification data"
- Removed claim of universal "three-level hierarchy"

**Conclusions section**:
- Changed: Removed "three-level hierarchy" language
- Added: "This single-region result is intriguing but cannot establish a universal three-level hierarchy"

---

### 2. Core Selection Justification Enhanced (Issue #8)

**Added explicit Monte Carlo migration bias justification**:

**Core selection criteria section now states**:
- "Protostellar migration bias: We performed a Monte Carlo simulation to quantify this effect, finding that the pairwise median statistic shows <0.01% bias for migration distances up to 0.05 pc"
- "Including all cores therefore provides the most statistically reliable measurement while maintaining consistency with previous HGBS analyses"

**Rationale**: The previous version mentioned the Monte Carlo simulation but didn't connect it explicitly to the core selection decision. This change makes the justification crystal clear.

---

### 3. Systematic Uncertainties Prominently Acknowledged (Credible #2)

**Added new dedicated subsection: "Systematic uncertainties in the measurement pipeline"**

**Five sources of systematic uncertainty now explicitly detailed**:

1. **DisPerSE persistence threshold sensitivity** - The $3σ choice affects which filaments are identified; not optimized for each region

2. **Manual editing subjectivity** - Inter-operator reproducibility not quantified; potential systematic differences

3. **Distance-dependent 2W threshold** - Physical association threshold varies with distance (30-102 px), potentially introducing distance-dependent bias

4. **Core catalog heterogeneity** - Different teams, slightly different procedures could contribute to 21% scatter

5. **Projection effects orientation uncertainty** - Correction factor ranges 1.18-1.41, changing 3D-corrected values from 3.3 to 3.9

**Key addition**: "These systematic uncertainties are difficult to quantify and propagate through the analysis. We acknowledge that they may contribute significantly to the observed scatter across regions (21% CV)... Future work with synthetic observations...could quantify the magnitude of these systematic effects."

---

### 4. Projection Uncertainty Emphasized (Credible #6)

**Before**: Mentioned that 3D range "encompasses the classical prediction"

**After**: Made prominent that projection uncertainty may fully explain the discrepancy:

**Projection correction section now states**:
- "Critical implication: Projection uncertainty may fully explain the apparent discrepancy"
- "The upper end of the 3D-corrected range (λ/W = 3.9) is statistically consistent with the classical prediction of 4.0"
- "This means that once projection effects are properly accounted for, there may be no discrepancy with classical theory at all"
- "The apparent sub-jeans spacing could be entirely a projection effect"

**Dual framework commitment**:
- 2D measurements as primary (directly observed)
- 3D-corrected values for theory comparison
- Explicit acknowledgment that projection uncertainty range overlaps with classical prediction

## What Was NOT Changed (and Why)

### Rejected Aggressive Demands

1. **"Fundamental mismatch between observable and theory"** - REJECTED
   - The referee demanded a "rigorous mapping" that no paper in this field provides
   - This is the standard HGBS comparison methodology used in 15+ papers
   - We cannot invalidate the entire literature based on one reviewer's demand

2. **"Pairwise median is physically meaningless"** - REJECTED  
   - The paper explicitly acknowledges all limitations
   - Provides NN measurements as a corrective
   - Pairwise median is required for comparability with HGBS literature
   - We cannot discard the established methodology of the field

3. **"NN analysis is not credible"** - ALREADY ADDRESSED
   - The paper already characterizes NN as "preliminary" with extensive limitations
   - The reviewer attacks the paper for flaws it itself identifies
   - We have now added explicit discussion of Ophiuchus anomaly

4. **"Reframe as: We don't know how to measure λ/W"** - REJECTED
   - This is philosophically valid but practically useless
   - The paper DOES measure λ/W using the best available methods
   - Acknowledging limitations doesn't mean we have NO measurement at all
