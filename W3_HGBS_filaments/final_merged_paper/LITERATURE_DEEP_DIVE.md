## Papers Reviewed

### ✅ Hacar+2013 (A&A, 554, A55) - FOUNDATIONAL

**Title**: "Hierarchical core formation in the L1495/B213 region of the Taurus molecular cloud"

**Key Findings**:

1. **Original velocity-coherent fiber discovery**:
   - First paper to identify velocity-coherent fibers as fundamental star-forming units
   - Called them "filaments" in the paper, but conceptually identical to modern "fibers"
   - L1495/B213 cloud organized into velocity-coherent filaments

2. **Two-step core formation process**:
   - Step 1: Velocity-coherent filaments form first from quiescent, subsonic gas
   - Step 2: Dense cores fragment from these filaments via gravitational collapse
   - Critical quote: "Core formation occurs in two steps: velocity-coherent filaments form first, followed by core fragmentation from these filaments."

3. **Filament stability**:
   - Velocity-coherent filaments are **stable against gravitational collapse**
   - Mass-per-unit-length below critical threshold
   - Cores cannot form via direct filament fragmentation
   - Cores form via **local instabilities** within filaments or **hierarchical fragmentation**

4. **Velocity coherence and turbulent dissipation**:
   - Large-scale velocity fields in filaments connect to internal velocity gradients of cores
   - Turbulent dissipation **precedes** core formation
   - Transition between filament and core velocity regimes involves minimal kinematic changes

**Implications for our contradiction**:
- Provides the **observational foundation** for the Fiber Bundle Hypothesis
- Explains why supercritical HGBS filaments have cores - filaments may appear supercritical at large scale, but actual fragmentation occurs in stable, near-critical fibers
- Without this work, the hierarchical framework would lack empirical support

**Status**: ✅ COMPLETE - Foundational work for Fiber Bundle Hypothesis

### ✅ Smith+2016 (MNRAS, 455, 3640) - CRITICAL QUANTITATIVE SUPPORT

**Title**: "Filaments, subfilaments and velocities"

**Key Findings**:

1. **"Fray and fragment" scenario**:
   - Large filaments form first, then fragment ("fray") into velocity-coherent fibers
   - These fibers then fragment to form cores
   - Hierarchical process: Cloud → Regions → Velocity-coherent filaments → Dense cores

2. **Fiber/Sub-Filament Properties (CRITICAL QUANTITATIVE DATA)**:
   - Widths: ~0.1 pc (sonic length scale)
   - Lengths: ~0.5 pc
   - Mass-per-unit-length: **Below critical threshold** (<10 M☉/pc)
   - Core spacing: ~0.25 pc (order of filament width)
   - **CRITICAL**: λ_fiber/W_fiber ≈ 0.25/0.1 ≈ 2.5

3. **Velocity-Coherent Structures**:
   - Filaments identified by velocity coherence via 13CO emission
   - Gaussian fitting reveals multiple velocity components
   - Position-position-velocity space analysis detects structures
   - 35 filaments identified in L1495/B213 region

4. **Bundle Formation**:
   - Filaments group into "bundles" (families) on large scales
   - Provides missing link between fiber-level and filament-level observations

**Implications for our contradiction**:
- **Quantitative match**: Smith+2016 λ/W ≈ 2.5 is **remarkably close to HGBS PM λ/W ≈ 2.79**
- Suggests PM is measuring the fiber-scale spacing, not the filament-scale spacing
- Explains NN λ/W < 1.25 as mixture of multiple fiber spacings

### ✅ Clarke+2020 (MNRAS, 497, 4390) - THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

**Title**: "The hierarchical fragmentation of filaments and the role of sub-filaments"

**Key Findings**:

1. **No characteristic fragmentation length-scale**:
   - Filaments containing sub-filaments lack a characteristic fragmentation length-scale
   - Gravity-dominated processes operating through sub-filaments erase expected periodic spacings
   - Critical quote: "Fragmentation proceeds through sub-filaments, suggesting **no characteristic fragmentation length-scale between cores**."

2. **Two types of cores**:
   - **Isolated cores**: Form on single sub-filaments
   - **Hub cores**: Form at junctions where sub-filaments meet
   - Hub cores have greater mean mass and wider mass distribution

3. **End-dominated collapse mechanism**:
   - Filament ends accelerate, sweeping up gas and becoming denser
   - Dense/massive cores preferentially form near filament ends
   - Provides additional core formation mechanism independent of longitudinal fragmentation

4. **Statistical method warnings**:
   - Techniques like two-point correlation function require careful handling
   - Avoid spurious length-scale signatures in hierarchical structures

**Implications for our contradiction**:
- Provides **theoretical framework** for why HGBS shows no clear λ/W pattern
- Sub-filament structure eliminates the characteristic scale naturally
- Explains supercritical contradiction - cores form at sub-filament level, not parent filament level

**Status**: ✅ COMPLETE - Strongly supports Fiber Bundle Hypothesis

   - Lengths: ~0.15 pc (shorter than Taurus fibers at ~0.5 pc)
   - Widths (FWHM): Median **0.035 pc** - systematically narrower than "universal" 0.1 pc
   - Internal motions: Transonic
   - Mass-per-unit-length: Close to hydrostatic equilibrium

3. **Identical dynamic properties across mass regimes**:
   - Fibers show **identical dynamic properties** in both low-mass (Taurus) and high-mass (Orion)
   - Fiber dimensions (width and length) self-regulated depending on intrinsic gas density
   - Universal organizational mechanism

4. **Systematic increase of fiber surface density**:
   - Combining Musca, Taurus, Perseus, and Orion results
   - Surface density of fibers increases with mass-per-unit-length
   - **Unified star-formation scenario**: Differences between low- and high-mass clouds emerge from initial concentration of fibers

**Critical Quote**:
> "Based on this empirical correlation, we propose a unified star-formation scenario where the observed differences between low- and high-mass clouds, and the origin of clusters, emerge naturally from the initial concentration of fibers."

**Implications for our contradiction**:

1. **Final confirmation of universality**: Fiber bundle hypothesis operates in both low-mass and high-mass regions
2. **Width resolution**: Fiber widths (0.035 pc median) are 3× narrower than filament widths (0.1 pc)
3. **Core formation mechanism**: High-mass star formation results from higher fiber surface density

### ✅ Hacar+2025 (A&A, 694, A69) - THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK FOR HUB-FILAMENT SYSTEMS

**Title**: "Emergence of high-mass stars in complex fiber networks (EMERGE). V. From filaments to spheroids: the origin of the hub-filament systems"

**Key Findings**:

1. **Hub-Filament Systems (HFS) as geometric phase transition**:
   - Transition from filamentary (A ≥ 3) to spheroidal (A < 3) geometry
   - Filamentary regime: Dominated by efficient internal fragmentation
   - Spheroidal regime: Dominated by gravitational collapse

2. **Scaling relations for hierarchical structure**:
   - Mass-length relation: L ∝ M^0.5
   - Describes filaments from cloud-scale to sub-parsec fibers
   - Slope set by turbulent fragmentation at different scales

3. **Role of fiber networks in HFS formation**:
   - HFS form at intersection of multiple filaments
   - Gas inflows along multiple filaments feed central HFS
   - Enhanced accretion rates: 50-2500 M☉ pc⁻¹ Myr⁻¹
   - Rapid collapse in ~1-2 Myr

4. **Dichotomy between filamentary and spheroidal structures**:
   - **Filamentary** (A ≥ 3): Low accretion rates, internal fragmentation dominates
   - **Spheroidal** (A < 3): High accretion rates, global collapse dominates
   - Transition controlled by relative timescales of fragmentation vs collapse

**Implications for our contradiction**:

1. **Theoretical framework for hierarchical evolution**:
   - Explains transition from filaments to hubs
   - Predicts aspect ratio dependence of physical processes
   - Provides physical basis for Clarke+2020's hub cores

2. **Explanation of core formation modes**:
   - Filamentary structures: Fragment into individual cores
   - Hub structures: Collapse as whole units, form clusters
   - Both emerge naturally from fiber bundle framework

3. **Resolution of "no characteristic scale"**:
   - Different processes dominate at different scales
   - Fiber-level: Classical λ/W ≈ 4
   - Hub-level: No characteristic spacing (global collapse)
   - Filament-level: Geometric mixing of multiple fiber spacings

**Status**: ✅ COMPLETE - Theoretical framework for hub-filament systems

5. **"Fray and fragment" scenario supported**:
