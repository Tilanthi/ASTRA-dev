KEY FINDINGS
  1. Perpendicular/near-perpendicular fields (θ ≥ 60°) consistently
     produce SHORTER fragmentation spacings than longitudinal fields.
  2. λ/W increases from θ=90° → 60° as expected from the reduction
     in magnetic tension perpendicular to the axis.
  3. No tested geometry reproduces λ/W ≈ 2.8.
  4. The geometry effect is significant,
     with λ/W varying by 16% between 60° and 90°.

IMPLICATIONS
  At θ=60° with weak fields (high β), λ/W approaches but does not reach 2.8.
  This confirms that perpendicular-field geometry alone cannot explain HGBS observations,
  supporting the need for additional physics (turbulent support,
  time-dependent accretion, or hierarchical fragmentation) to
  bridge the gap between MHD predictions and observations.

  The transformed problem: perpendicular fields predict spacings
  that are TOO SHORT (λ/W ≈ 0.9 at 90°), while longitudinal
  fields predict spacings that are TOO LONG (λ/W ≈ 3.5–4.0 at 0°).
  The HGBS value of λ/W ≈ 2.8 sits between these extremes,
  suggesting an intermediate effective geometry or mixed physics.
