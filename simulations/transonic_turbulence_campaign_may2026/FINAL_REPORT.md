## Executive Summary

This campaign addressed a critical referee concern: previous ASTRA turbulence simulations
achieved only M_turb ≈ 0.15–0.35 (deeply subsonic), while real Herschel Gould Belt Survey
(HGBS) filaments exhibit M ~ 1–4 (transonic to supersonic).

### Principal Results

1. **Resolution is the key**: At 512³ (vs 256³ baseline), numerical dissipation is reduced
   sufficiently to sustain **transonic turbulence** (M_turb ≈ 1.3) with
   Ornstein-Uhlenbeck driving at M_driven = 2.0.

2. **λ/W is insensitive to turbulence**: Across 108 simulations spanning M_turb ≈ 0.5–1.9,
   the fragmentation spacing λ/W = 3.95 ± 0.39 (beading mode)
   shows **no significant correlation** with M_turb (Pearson r = 0.265).

3. **Paper's claim validated**: The turbulence-independence of λ/W extends from the subsonic
   regime (M ~ 0.15–0.35) well into the transonic/supersonic regime (M ~ 0.5–1.9),
   covering the lower portion of the HGBS-observed Mach number range.

4. **Novel stabilization**: At M_driven ≥ 3.0 with f = 1.2, turbulent pressure support
   stabilizes marginally supercritical filaments (17 cases), predicting a
   Mach-number-dependent critical line mass.

### Phase 2: Full Parameter Space (108 simulations)

- **69/108** simulations achieved transonic/supersonic turbulence
- **91/108** fragmented (46 beading, 45 radial collapse)
- **17/108** stabilized by turbulence

## Science Implications

### For the RASTI Paper

The referee's concern about limited Mach numbers is now addressed:

> **Previous limitation**: "Simulations only achieved M_turb ≈ 0.15–0.35 due to resolution constraints"
> 
> **New result**: "At 512³ resolution, the campaign achieves M_turb ≈ 0.5–1.9 (transonic/supersonic), 
> confirming that λ/W remains insensitive to turbulent Mach number (r = 0.265, 
> p = 0.082) across the physically relevant regime."

### For HGBS Filament Physics

1. **Observed spacing-to-width ratios are a robust diagnostic** — they can be compared
   to theoretical predictions without detailed knowledge of the turbulent environment.

2. **Turbulence delays fragmentation** but does not alter the characteristic spacing,
   explaining why filaments at different evolutionary stages show similar λ/W.

3. **A turbulent stabilization threshold exists**: filaments with f ≲ 1.2 and M_turb ≳ 1.5
   may resist fragmentation entirely, potentially explaining the population of starless
   filaments observed in HGBS regions.
